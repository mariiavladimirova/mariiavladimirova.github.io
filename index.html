About

I am a senior research scientist at Criteo AI Lab in Paris and a member of the Fairplay joint team (Inria). My work spans fairness in machine learning, causal inference, and constrained optimization.

My research addresses fairness and ethics across the ML stack: bidding systems, recommender systems on tabular data, and large generative models. I study how evaluation protocol choices drive fairness verdicts as much as model properties do, and promote reproducible auditing through Fairness Cards. On the decision side, I use causal inference and uplift modeling to learn personalized treatment policies for large-scale online systems, with a focus on constrained allocation and budget-aware optimization. Before joining Criteo, I did research on theoretical deep learning at Inria Grenoble 🚡🏔️. An overview of current and upcoming research directions is available on the research page.

I provide technical and scientific inputs to policymakers on AI regulation, including contributions to CNIL consultations on GDPR and AI systems in the context of the EU AI Act. I also co-chair annual AI symposiums, give invited talks at universities and summer schools, and was recognized as a Rising Star in AI Ethics 2025.

Selected Publications

  • Fairness Failure in Generative Models is an Evaluation Problem
    Mariia Vladimirova, Jean-Yves Franceschi, Thibaut Issenhuth
    ICML Position Paper 2026
    Project page HAL Code & data
    This position paper argues that fairness failures in generative models are ultimately an evaluation problem: fairness findings are rarely comparable across papers or actionable for deployment decisions. We diagnose recurring empirical and conceptual failure modes and propose Fairness Cards as a minimal reporting artifact that makes evaluation choices explicit (covering prompt protocols, decoding settings, refusal handling, and scorer details), enabling reproducibility, comparability, and accountability.
    @inproceedings{vladimirova2026fairness, title={Position: Fairness Failure in Generative Models is an Evaluation Problem}, author={Vladimirova, Mariia and Franceschi, Jean-Yves and Issenhuth, Thibaut}, booktitle={International Conference on Machine Learning}, year={2026} }
  • FairJob: A Real-World Dataset for Fairness in Online Systems
    Mariia Vladimirova, Eustache Diemert, Federico Pavone
    NeurIPS Datasets & Benchmarks, 2024
    PDF Dataset Blog post
    We present FairJob, a real-world dataset for studying fairness in job recommendation within online advertising. A key contribution is a pragmatic method for obtaining proxy estimates of protected attributes that are not directly observable, while maintaining predictive power and preserving a realistic benchmark. Experimental results demonstrate potential improvements in fairness and the associated trade-offs with utility when applying bias mitigation strategies.
    @inproceedings{vladimirova2024fairjob, title={{FairJob}: A Real-World Dataset for Fairness in Online Systems}, author={Vladimirova, Mariia and Diemert, Eustache and Pavone, Federico}, booktitle={Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems, Datasets and Benchmarks Track}, year={2024} }
  • A primer on Bayesian neural networks: review and debates
    Julyan Arbel, Konstantinos Pitas, Mariia Vladimirova, Vincent Fortuin
    Statistical Science 2026 (accepted 2025)
    PDF
    A review of Bayesian neural networks covering their theoretical foundations, approximate inference methods, and practical applications, alongside a discussion of open debates in the field regarding scalability, prior choice, and calibration.
